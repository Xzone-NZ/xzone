The loop

From a thought to a post

Good content ideas turn up everywhere: a customer question at the market, a comment on a Reel, a review, something you saw on a walk. The Ideas database catches them all, and once a week a scheduled review in Claude reads them and tells the team what to make next, who should make it and in what format.

1. Drop itAnyone adds the idea to Notion, Head of Marketing, Ideas. Status starts as New.
2. Weekly reviewThe scheduled task reads every New idea, checks the content plan and what's trending.
3. DecideThe team reads the review and agrees what to shortlist, park or make now.
4. Make itUse the prompts in the Prompting guide. The idea moves to Planned.
5. UsedOnce live, the idea is marked Used and linked to the finished piece.

Where it lives

Notion, Head of Marketing, Ideas. On your phone, open the Notion app, find Ideas (star it so it's on your home screen) and tap New.

The database was seeded with 41 ideas from the summer calendar, so it's never empty.

Who can add ideas

Everyone. Jess, Dan, the team, and the Marketing Project itself, which adds ideas from comments, story replies, reviews and results.

There is no such thing as too small. A single line is enough to start.

What to drop in

Customer questions

"Can I use it before a run?" Every question is a post, a story or a FAQ.

Reviews and messages

Paste them word for word with the name as shown. They become proof posts.

Moments

An event, a season, a race, a stockist visit, a new photo you took.

Behind the scenes

Making a batch, a new label, Jess with the horses, Dan's guitar.

Things you saw

A format or trend that would suit us. Add the link.

Results

"The ankle carousel got 40 saves." Ideas to repeat what works.

Quality and status

What makes a good idea

Thin

"Post about knees"

No angle, no source, no reason it matters now. The review can only say "needs more detail".

Strong

"Three customers this month asked if Joints Rub is OK before gardening. Quick Reel: Jess rubs it in, then pulls weeds. Spring/summer gardening season."

Real question, a format, a person, a reason for now. Ready to shortlist.

A strong idea answers four questions

1
What is it? The topic and angle in one line.
2
Why now? A season, event, question or trend.
3
Where did it come from? A real customer beats a hunch.
4
Who could front it? Jess, Dan, a customer, hands only.
Check before you add: no new offers (only Jess and Dan set those), no health promises ("cures arthritis" can't become content), and no customer's private details. Public reviews are fine with the name as shown.

How an idea moves

StatusMeansWho moves it

Parked isn't deleted. Seasonal ideas come back round, and the review will flag parked ideas when their season returns.

The Ideas database and weekly review04
Once a week

What the weekly review tells you

The scheduled task runs once a week and posts a short report. Read it at the start of the week and spend 15 minutes agreeing what to make. It covers six things.

1Do we need more content?

Checks the next two weeks against the plan: 3 posts a week plus 1 email, and the monthly mix. Shows what's drafted, what's approved and where the gaps are.

2New ideas, reviewed

Every New idea gets a call: shortlist, park, merge with a similar one, or needs more detail. With the format, range and pillar it suits.

3Who should make it

Names who's needed for each piece: the Project to draft, Jess or Dan on camera, someone to build in Canva or film, a customer or ambassador.

4What type

Recommends the format for each idea (a question is often a Reel or carousel, a review a proof post) and the skill to use.

5What's trending

NZ seasons, sport and events coming up, wellness and natural health topics people are searching, and formats working on Instagram and Facebook.

6Balance check

Flags if we're light on a range or pillar, too salesy (80/20), or repeating ourselves. Ends with this week's 3 to 5 actions.

What a review looks like (example)

Content health: Week 3 has 2 of 3 posts drafted, no Reel yet. Email for 9 Dec not started. Monthly mix: Reels 1 of 4, carousels 2 of 4.
New ideas (4): "Warm-up before backyard cricket": shortlist as the week 5 Reel, Sports, How to use. Dan on camera, film at a family game. "Gardening knees": merge with the existing "Kneeling in the garden" idea. "Free shipping over $50": park, offers come from Jess and Dan.
Trending: Great Walks season opens; tramping prep content searches up. Boxing Day cricket. Summer heat and staying active early in the day.
Actions: 1. Shortlist the cricket Reel. 2. Book 20 minutes with Dan to film. 3. Draft the 9 Dec newsletter on kawakawa.

For whoever manages the scheduled task

The weekly review prompt

Your scheduled task is already set up. Compare it with this version and copy across anything it's missing, so every review covers the same six points. Run it inside the Marketing Project with Notion and GitHub switched on, and web search on for the trends.

Weekly review promptRun the weekly content ideas review for X-ZONE. 1. Load the Role Card, Business Context and Brand Voice & Caption Guide from Notion (Head of Marketing), and brief/06-brand-spec.md and calendar/ from GitHub Xzone-NZ/xzone. 2. Content health: compare the next two weeks of the Output Log and calendar against the targets (3 posts a week cross-posted to Facebook and Instagram, 1 email a week; monthly 4 Reels, 4 single posts, 4 carousels, 12 Facebook posts, 3 newsletters, 1 campaign email). List what's drafted, approved and missing. 3. New ideas: for every idea in the Ideas database with Status New, recommend Shortlist, Park (with a reason), Merge (with which idea) or Needs detail. For each one to shortlist give the format, skill, range, pillar, a hook, a suggested week, and who is needed (Marketing Project, Jess, Dan, the team member building assets, an ambassador or customer). Fill in any blank fields. Do not change Status yourself. 4. Backlog: flag Shortlisted ideas older than 3 weeks, and Parked ideas whose season has come back. 5. Trending: search the web for what's coming up or trending in the next 4 weeks for a NZ natural pain relief brand: NZ seasons, sport and outdoor events, natural health and wellness topics, and formats working on Instagram and Facebook. Suggest up to 5 content ideas from it, add them to Ideas as New (Source: Head of Marketing), and say why each fits. 6. Balance: flag gaps by range and pillar, and whether the mix keeps to 80% educate, 20% sell. 7. End with this week's 3 to 5 actions, each with an owner. Rules: never suggest an offer Jess and Dan haven't set; anything on te reo Māori or rongoā rākau needs cultural review; keep to the Claims rules. Keep the report under 400 words. Add one line to the Activity Log.

Good to know

  • The review recommends; people decide. It does not shortlist or park on its own.
  • It can only see trends if web search is on for the task.
  • If Notion or GitHub isn't connected when it runs, it should say so and stop.

Weekly 15-minute routine

  1. Read the review.
  2. Agree shortlist and park calls.
  3. Book anyone needed on camera.
  4. Ask the Project to plan the week.
  5. Send the approval batch to Jess and Dan.
