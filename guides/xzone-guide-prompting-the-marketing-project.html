How the Marketing Project works

The Marketing Project in Claude acts as X-ZONE's Head of Marketing. It already knows the brand, the products, the claims rules and the summer calendar. Your job is to tell it what you want, for which range, when it goes out and what to work from. It does the rest, as a draft for Jess and Dan.

What happens every time you ask for content

1. Loads contextReads the Role Card, Business Context and Caption Guide in Notion, plus the brand spec and calendar on GitHub.
2. Picks a skillFinds the matching playbook (Reels, Stories, Campaign Email...) and follows its steps.
3. Checks IdeasLooks in the Ideas database for shortlisted ideas that fit.
4. DraftsWrites the copy, shot list or email, then runs Claims Check.
5. Logs itSaves the piece to the Output Log as "Awaiting approval" for Jess and Dan.

The five-part prompt

Every good request has these five parts. Leave one out and the Project will either ask you or guess. A guess is slower than a sentence.

PartWhat to sayExample
1. The formatReel, carousel, story, newsletter... Naming the skill is even better."Use the Reels skill to plan a Reel"
2. The rangeRelief, Joints, Sports, or brand (all ranges)."for Joints"
3. WhenCalendar week or the go-live date."for week 3, Wednesday 9 Dec"
4. Work fromAn idea from the Ideas database, a review, a photo, a customer question, notes."from the idea 'Hands that work hard'"
5. Anything specialWho is on camera, an offer Jess and Dan have set, a link, a deadline."Jess is on camera, filmed in the garden"
The templateUse the [skill name] skill to create a [format] for [range], going out [week / date]. Work from [the idea, review, photo or notes]. [Anything special: who is on camera, the offer as Jess and Dan set it, the link to use].
How to prompt the Marketing Project02
Before you type

Seven rules that save time

1
One chat per job. Start a new chat in the Marketing Project for each piece or weekly batch. Long chats get muddled and slow.
2
Name the skill. "Use the Stories skill" removes guesswork. The full list is on page 12.
3
Paste the raw material. Paste the review word for word, attach the photo, copy the customer's question. The Project only quotes what it can see.
4
Never ask it to invent an offer. Sales, codes, bundles and free shipping come from Jess and Dan only. Give the exact wording or it will stop and ask.
5
Point to an idea. If the idea is in the Ideas database, give its title. The Project will link the finished piece back to it.
6
Be specific with changes. "Shorter, warmer opening, swap the photo for the knee shot" beats "make it better".
7
Nothing goes out unapproved. Everything is a draft until Jess and Dan approve it. Then it is scheduled in Meta Business Suite or built in Shopify Messaging.
The rules it always follows for you: NZ spelling. No emoji. No exclamation marks in headlines. "Supports", "helps", "traditionally used", never "cures" or "proven". Lab results are always "in independent lab tests". Name the herbs. Write as "we". 80% educate, 20% sell. Headlines are hooks. You do not need to repeat these in your prompt.

Where things live

WhatWhere
The Marketing Projectclaude.ai, Projects, Marketing. Always start chats from inside the Project so it has its instructions and connectors.
Ideas databaseNotion, Head of Marketing, Ideas. Drop every idea here (see the Ideas guide).

If the Project says a connector failed

It will stop rather than work from memory. Check Notion and GitHub are switched on for the Project (the tools menu in the chat box), then ask again with "Try again now the connectors are on".

How to prompt the Marketing Project03
Social

Weekly set and single posts

Weekly social content set

The week's three Facebook and Instagram posts, planned together and handed to the channel skills.

Weekly Social Content Set

Give it

  • The calendar week
  • Any idea titles you want used
  • Range focus, if any
  • Anything happening that week (event, sale set by Jess and Dan)

You get back

A week plan (which format on which day), then each post drafted in full: hook, design notes, caption, hashtags, Claims Check, one Output Log row each.

Copy this promptUse the Weekly Social Content Set skill to plan week [number] ([dates]). Check the calendar first for anything already drafted. Pull from Shortlisted ideas in the Ideas database, and use [idea titles] if they fit. Range focus: [Relief / Joints / Sports / mixed]. Give me the week plan, then draft each piece with its channel skill.

Then try

  • "Swap Friday's post for a testimonial, using this review: [paste]"
  • "Show me the three hooks side by side before you write the captions"

Instagram single post

One feed image, 1080 x 1350, cross-posted to Facebook.

Instagram Posts

Give it

  • The topic or idea
  • Range
  • The photo you want (file name or attach it)
  • Go-live date

You get back

A hook headline with its highlight word, layout notes (Post A, B or C style), caption of 60 to 160 words, up to 5 hashtags, Claims Check result.

Copy this promptUse the Instagram Posts skill to create a single post for [range] going out [date]. Topic: [what it is about, or the idea title]. Photo: [file name from brief/photos, or attached]. Give me the hook, the design layout to build in Canva, the caption and hashtags.

Then try

  • "Give me three other hook options"
  • "Make it white ground with navy text, it's a Joints post"
How to prompt the Marketing Project04
Social

Carousels and Reels

Carousel

Multi-slide teaching posts: how-tos, herb guides, myth busters. Up to 7 slides.

Instagram Posts

Give it

  • The lesson you want to teach
  • Range
  • Number of slides (default 6 to 7)
  • Photos for the cover

You get back

Slide-by-slide text (cover hook, one idea per slide, a save/share last slide), design notes per slide, caption, hashtags, Claims Check.

Copy this promptUse the Instagram Posts skill to create a [number]-slide carousel for [range], going out [date]. The lesson: [e.g. what to do in the first 48 hours after a rolled ankle]. Cover photo: [file name or attached]. Give me the cover hook, the words for each slide, the caption and the design notes.

Then try

  • "Cut it to 5 slides and keep the strongest points"
  • "Turn slide 3 into a simple numbered list"

Reel

Top priority format: 4 a month, on Wednesdays. 15 to 30 seconds.

Reels

Give it

  • The type: how-to, herb of the week, founders, first aid moment
  • Who is on camera (Jess, Dan, a customer)
  • Where you will film
  • Range and date

You get back

Cover hook, subline and "Watch..." play line; a 4 to 8 shot list with time codes and on-screen text; end card; caption; shoot note with permissions to confirm.

Copy this promptUse the Reels skill to plan a [type] Reel for [range], going out [date]. Topic: [what it shows]. On camera: [Jess / Dan / hands only / customer name]. Location: [where]. Give me the cover hook, a shot list with on-screen words, the caption and a shoot note.

Then try

  • "We only have 10 minutes to film. Simplify the shot list to 4 shots on a phone"
  • "Write Dan's voiceover script for this, in his words"
Filming tip: the first 3 seconds must show the problem (the sore knee, the stiff hands). Natural light, real NZ places, no stock. Only Jess or Dan do voiceovers.
How to prompt the Marketing Project05
Social

Stories and Facebook

Stories

Polls, question boxes, quizzes and link stickers. Full photo plus a two-option poll and a "tomorrow we share..." line.

Stories

Give it

  • What you want to learn from followers, or what you are teasing
  • Number of frames
  • Photos
  • Date

You get back

Each frame's words, sticker type and options, photo, and run notes (what to post the next day with the answers).

Copy this promptUse the Stories skill to make a [number]-frame story set for [date]. Goal: [e.g. find out which joint bothers people most, to plan next week's carousel]. Photos: [file names or attached]. Include a poll or question sticker and a "tomorrow we share..." line.

Then try

  • "Turn the poll results into a carousel idea and add it to Ideas"
  • "Make a 3-frame quiz on the native herbs in the Joints range"

Facebook post, poll or Live

Community posts, Facebook-only polls and run sheets for a Facebook Live with Jess and Dan.

Facebook Posts

Give it

  • The type: cross-post, community post, poll or Live
  • Topic
  • For a Live: date, time, who hosts, length

You get back

A Facebook caption (longer and chattier than Instagram), or poll options, or a Live run sheet with timings, talking points and safe answers to likely questions.

Copy this promptUse the Facebook Posts skill to create a [community post / poll / Live run sheet] for [date]. Topic: [what it is about]. [For a Live: Jess and Dan host, [length] minutes, at [time]. Questions people have asked: [paste].] Write it for our Facebook audience, which skews older than Instagram.

Then try

  • "Turn this week's Instagram carousel into a Facebook post"
  • "Add three questions we can expect and approved ways to answer them"
Health questions in comments or Lives: the Project never answers "will this help my condition". It drafts a kind reply that points people to their GP or pharmacist, and flags it for Jess and Dan.
How to prompt the Marketing Project06
Email

Newsletters and sale emails

Weekly newsletter

The weekly email to subscribers via Shopify Messaging: one lesson, one proof point, one product link.

Campaign Email Sequence

Give it

  • The topic (a herb, a pain topic, a how-to)
  • A review to quote, if you have one
  • The product link
  • Send date

You get back

Subject line plus an alternative, preview text, short body, button text and link, suggested social posts to match, Claims Check.

Copy this promptUse the Campaign Email Sequence skill to write this week's newsletter, sending [date]. Topic: [the one lesson]. Proof point: [paste a Judge.me review word for word, with the name as shown]. Product link: [URL]. Give me two subject lines, preview text, the body and the button.

Then try

  • "Make the subject line a curiosity hook, under 45 characters"
  • "Now build it as a designed email with the Shopify E-Newsletter skill"

Sale campaign (3 emails)

The build-up to a sale moment, modelled on Labour Day Buy 2 Get 1 Free: tease, reasons to believe, offer day.

Campaign Email Sequence

Give it

  • The offer, word for word, as Jess and Dan set it
  • Start and end dates and times
  • Range focus
  • Segment, if any

You get back

Email 1 (tease, 3 to 5 days out), Email 2 (customer story and the approved lab line, day before), Email 3 (offer day, one button), each with subjects and preview text, plus social support.

Copy this promptUse the Campaign Email Sequence skill to write the 3-email build-up for [campaign name]. Offer (set by Jess and Dan): [exact wording]. Runs: [start date and time] to [end date and time]. Range focus: [range]. Segment: [all subscribers / past Joints buyers / ...]. Also suggest the matching social posts and stories.

Then try

  • "Write a last-chance email for the final 6 hours"
  • "Give me story frames counting down to the sale"
No offer, no campaign. If the offer is not confirmed in writing by Jess and Dan, the Project stops and asks. Offers so far: the old-price hold after the November price rise and the mid-December pre-Christmas sale.

Email and proof

Designed emails and reviews

Designed HTML email

Turns an approved email into a ready-to-paste, branded custom-coded email for Shopify Messaging.

Shopify E-Newsletter

Give it

  • The approved email (or its Output Log row)
  • The hero photo
  • Product links

You get back

HTML in the X-ZONE layout (green header, lime highlights, Poppins/Arial), a preview to check, and notes on anything to test before sending.

Copy this promptUse the Shopify E-Newsletter skill to build the approved email "[email name]" as a designed HTML email. Hero photo: [file name or attached]. Product links: [URLs]. Give me the code to paste into Shopify Messaging and a preview.

Then try

  • "Send me a test version with the product block moved above the review"
  • "Check it on a narrow phone screen and fix anything that wraps badly"

Testimonial graphic

Turns a public review into a proof post. Reviews are quoted in full, word for word.

Testimonial to Graphic

Give it

  • The review, pasted exactly as it appears
  • The reviewer's name as shown
  • Where it is from (Judge.me, Google) and the product

You get back

Quote layout (photo on top, review in a band below), caption that lets the customer do the selling, design brief, Claims Check (a review can still contain a claim we cannot repeat).

Copy this promptUse the Testimonial to Graphic skill to turn this review into a post for [date]: "[paste the review exactly]" [Name as shown], Verified review, [product], from [Judge.me / Google Reviews]. Give me the quote layout, caption and design brief.

Then try

  • "Find our three best Joints reviews in the Ideas database and draft all three"
  • "Make a story version of this review"

Long form

Ambassadors and blogs

Brand ambassador post

The "Introducing: Name, Sport" series: blog post, 3-slide Instagram carousel and Facebook post.

Brand Ambassador Posts

Give it

  • The ambassador's Q&A answers, pasted in full
  • Their photos (attach them)
  • The publish date

You get back

A proofread blog post in their own words, a 3-slide carousel (black and red ambassador style), a Facebook post, a mock-up and a list of flags to confirm with the ambassador.

Copy this promptUse the Brand Ambassador Posts skill. Here is [name]'s Q&A for the Introducing series, publishing [date]: [paste their answers] Photos attached. Give me the blog post, the carousel, the Facebook post and anything to check with them.

Then try

  • "Write a short message asking [name] to confirm the flagged points"
  • "Pull one quote from the blog for a story"

Blog post

Monthly blog for xzone.nz. There is no Blog skill yet, so the Project works from the Role Card and Business Context and will say so.

No skill yet

Give it

  • The topic and the search phrase people would type
  • Rough length (800 to 1,200 words is typical)
  • Products to link

You get back

Title, meta description, the post, social posts to promote it, Claims Check, and a suggestion on turning it into a skill.

Copy this promptThere is no Blog skill yet, so use the Role Card, Business Context and Caption Guide. Write a blog post for xzone.nz on [topic], aimed at people searching "[search phrase]". About [length] words. Teach first, product last. Link to [products]. Give me a title, meta description, the post with subheadings, and 3 social posts to share it. Then tell me whether this should become a skill.

Then try

  • "Yes, draft a Blog skill from this and add it to the Skills page for Jess and Dan to approve"

Monthly marketing report

First week of each month: revenue, ads, email and social results against the $40k and $80k targets.

Monthly Marketing Report

Give it

  • The month
  • Any numbers the Project cannot see: Shopify sales, Meta and Google ad results, email results (paste or attach exports)

You get back

A report page in Notion: headline numbers, what worked, what to stop, next month's plan, and ideas added to the Ideas database.

Copy this promptUse the Monthly Marketing Report skill for [month]. Here are the numbers: [paste Shopify revenue, ad spend and results, email opens and revenue, or attach the exports]. Compare to target and last month, show what worked and what didn't, and recommend next month's focus.

Then try

  • "Which three posts drove the most saves and shares? Add similar ideas to Ideas"
How to prompt the Marketing Project10
After the first draft

Feedback, changes and sign-off

Most pieces need one round of changes. Say exactly what to change, and when Jess and Dan give feedback, tell the Project so it learns the rule for next time.

Asking for changes

Revise a draftChanges to [piece name]: - Hook: [shorter / more curious / use the word "..."]. - Caption: [cut to under 100 words / warmer opening]. - Photo: swap to [file name]. Keep everything else the same and update the Output Log row.
Get optionsGive me [3] different options for the [hook / subject line / opening line], each a different angle.

Passing on Jess and Dan's feedback

FeedbackJess and Dan's feedback on [piece name]: "[paste their words]". Apply it, set the Output Log status to [Approved / Changes needed], and if this should be a permanent rule, suggest where to add it (Role Card, the skill, or the Caption Guide).

Once it's approved

Scheduled[Piece name] is approved and scheduled in Meta Business Suite for [date and time]. Set the Output Log row to Scheduled and mark the idea it came from as Planned.
Results[Piece name] went live on [date]. Results after [7] days: [reach, plays, saves, shares, comments / opens, clicks, orders]. Add these to the Output Log row, mark the idea as Used, and tell me what to repeat.

Getting a batch ready for approval

Approval batchList everything in the Output Log that is Awaiting approval, as one short message I can send to Jess and Dan, with the link to each piece.

When something goes wrong

It...Do this
says a connector failedTurn Notion and GitHub on in the chat's tools menu, then "Try again".
asks about an offerGet the exact offer from Jess and Dan. It will not invent one.
flags an open questionIt is in brief/open-questions.md. Ask Jess and Dan, then tell the Project the answer.
uses the wrong photoGive the file name from brief/photos, or attach the photo.
sounds genericPaste a real review, customer question or detail. Specifics make the copy.
repeats an old post"Check the Output Log and calendar for anything similar first."
forgets to log it"Write this back to the Output Log and Activity Log now."
Got an idea, not a request? Drop it in the Ideas database instead. The weekly review will tell you if and when to make it, and who should. See the Ideas guide.
