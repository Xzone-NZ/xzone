What we're trying to do

The job for summer

Make X-ZONE the account Kiwis save when something hurts. Summer is when the injuries happen: touch, cricket, surf, tramping, gardening, the long drive to the bach. If the grid teaches people what to do in the first 48 hours, the product sells itself in step three.

Why 80/20 is the whole strategy

Their current grid is price tags and product shots, and it has no consistency. Saves and shares are what the algorithm rewards, and nobody saves a price. Teaching content earns the reach; the one promo a week spends it.

Targets for the quarter

  • Saves per post up 3x on the current average by week 6. The single best measure of educational value.
  • Reach up 50% month on month, driven by Reels.
  • Instagram followers up 25%; Facebook page likes up 15%.
  • Link clicks from the price-hold and pre-Christmas sale pieces tracked with UTM links, so the 20% proves its worth.
  • Story replies and DMs up, because the question-box format gives next quarter's content.

Two platforms, two audiences

Instagram: the athlete and the outdoors

Younger, visual, scrolling Reels. Gets the full cadence: three feed posts, two Reels, stories most days. Grid alternates green-ground posts and photo-led posts so it reads as one brand (see the brand guidelines).

Facebook: parents, tradies, gift buyers, rural NZ

Older, more likely to buy as a gift or for the family. Every Instagram post and Reel is reshared, plus one Facebook-only community post each Thursday: a poll, a question thread, a stockist shout-out, a customer review. The Relief range and gift content lead here. The Live Q&A is a Facebook event.

Three ranges, not one. What the FAQ changes

The FAQ at xzone.nz corrects an assumption we'd been making. There are three ranges, each with its own set of native herbs, and the eleven are spread across them. No single jar holds all eleven. The plan's herb series still works, but every herb post now names which range it lives in.

ReliefRub, roll-on, spray. Mid-strength, for everyday aches, bruises, sprains, growing pains, headaches. Suitable for children over five. Herbs: Harakeke, Horopito, Kahikatea, Rātā, Koromiko.
JointsRub only. Higher strength, for stiffness and mobility. Not for children under seven. Herbs: Tūpākihi, Kūmarahou, Pukatea, Karaeo.
SportsRub, roll-on, spray. Higher strength, for warm-up and recovery. Not for children under seven. Herbs: Harakeke, Kūmarahou, Kōwhai, Pukatea, Pūriri.

The lab results, told carefully

In August 2024 Trinity Bioactives, an independent contract lab, tested the rubs against human macrophages (cells active in pain and inflammation) and two herbs against the COX-2 pathway. It is the brand's best proof and the website buries it. It carries the week 7 recovery-science carousel and gets one slide in every herb post for Pūriri and Tūpākihi.

54.1%Sports Rub: reduction in macrophage concentration. The headline number.
48.1%Joints Rub
42.7%Relief Rub
28.4%Tūpākihi oil: COX-2 reduction in the same independent lab test
14.2%Pūriri tincture: COX-2 reduction
  • Week 7 Wed carousel, five slides: what a macrophage is, what the lab did, the three numbers, the two herbs, where to read the full page.
  • Week 10 Tue Reel (Tūpākihi) and week 6 Tue Reel (Pūriri) each get one on-screen line with their COX-2 number.
  • Stories: a three-frame "the science bit" set saved to a highlight, re-shared under every promo post so the 20% always sits on proof.
  • Facebook: the week 7 post is the one to boost. Science content travels well with the older audience.
Wording rule. These are in-vitro results on cells, not a clinical trial on people. Say "in independent lab tests, the Sports Rub reduced pain-associated cells by 54%" and link the page. Never "proven", and never compare the products with a medicine such as indomethacin. Always lead with "in independent lab tests".

It feeds the Monday carousel, the Wednesday post and the story link, and it builds the search traffic that social can't. Six hundred to nine hundred words, one herb or one injury per piece, a plain "when to see a doctor" line where it applies.

Each article is written in the first two weeks of the month in a batch, so nothing is written the night before. Herbs three to ten get their own short articles in the autumn plan, each linking back to the cornerstone guide from week 11.

What it takes to run

From Jess and Dan

  • One filming half-day a month: six to eight Reels shot in a batch (bush, workshop, a club training, on site with Dan).
  • Eleven herb fact sheets, three facts each, checked against the label and the independent test.
  • One blog article a week, 600 to 900 words. Reclaim Co drafts, Jess and Dan check the facts and sign off. About two hours of their time a month.
  • Customer photos and reviews with permission, collected through the Thursday Facebook posts.
  • Twenty minutes for the Live in week 10.

Tools

  • Buffer runs the schedule. Connect X-ZONE's Instagram and Facebook as channels (two channels, so the plan above the free tier), set the posting schedule to 7am and 7pm NZT, and queue a fortnight at a time. The 78 pieces live on the Buffer Ideas board in three columns, one per month, and get dragged into the queue once the photo or Reel is ready.
  • Reels: Buffer schedules them, but check the first two post correctly with the cover frame. If covers drop, post Reels natively and use Buffer for the reminder.
  • UTM links on every promo piece so sales can be traced back to the 20%.

Budget, kept small

  • NZ$300 a month in boosts, spent only on the educational post with the most saves each week. Teaching content is what earns new followers cheaply.
  • Behind the price-hold and pre-Christmas sale posts, retargeting people who engaged with the lessons that preceded them.

How we'll know it's working

  • Weekly: saves, shares, Reel reach, story replies. Fifteen minutes every Monday.
  • Monthly: follower growth, link clicks per promo, best and worst three posts, and one change to the plan.
  • Week 13: full review, then the autumn plan built from what people saved and asked.
Two things to confirm with the client before week 1. The herb facts need checking against the label and any claims Jess and Dan are comfortable with. And the Waitangi-week rongoā post should be reviewed by someone with the right knowledge before it goes out, with credit given properly.
